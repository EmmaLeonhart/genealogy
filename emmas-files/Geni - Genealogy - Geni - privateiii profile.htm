About

I'm a Chinese ex-Curator. My personal interests started with the Manchus, but of course I'm open to any Chinese region or period of time. For now I'd like to focus on notable Chinese (including Chinese Americans) in the last 150 years, and their lineages as far back as possible, in the hope to attract more collaboration and interests.

More recently I started editing profiles en masse on capitalization issues (e.g. SMITH -> Smith) with a "bot" script, no information being added or removed whatsoever. On a World Family Tree, it is better to adopt a consistent style (mind you that you can set your personal display preferences here); but more importantly, it is to save countless man-hours (mostly of fellow curators) that could be better spent on more meaningful tasks. In Februrary 2019 alone, the bot has edited 160,000+ profiles. (If you only manage/have added 20 profiles, you might want to redirect your complaints to Customer Service.)

Sources to help you get started:

http://familysearch.org is building an impressive collection of genealogical records (宗譜), from various sources around the world, including the Columbia University collection.

http://archive.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/ttsweb/html_name/search.php collects biographical information from the Ming and Qing dynasties, based on the imperial archive (now preserved in Taiwan).

http://digitalarchives.tw/ is another Taiwan site with a lot of high-resolution digital images of primary sources cited in the search portal above.

Last but not least, a particular type of semi-official print documents, known as 同年齒錄, contains quite a lot of genealogical information. Those in the Harvard University collection has been digitized by (and available on) Google Books. Libraries across China should hold many copies from the 19th century, if one is willing to go out and look for them. Given that many genealogical records have been destroyed during the Culture Revolution, this could be one of the few ways that one might retrieve their lost genealogy.
