One issue that we do have with our data is that some people have patronyms but no surnames. Some people have surnames but no patronyms. Some people have middle names, have first name, middle name, patronym. Some people have first name, patronym, given name.

What is in the surname lit? The surname thing on geni is not always something that clearly corresponds to a surname versus a patronym particularly. We have to check in the given names and in the surname whether it is a patronym or the regular name.

There are probably going to be edge cases and for the edge cases I am going to want you to tell me about the edge cases. Do an ask-user question on the edge cases so that I can figure them out.

A PIPE IN AN IMPORTED LABEL -- ruled 2026-09-09, all three shapes
================================================================

1,640 imported labels carry a `|`. Three shapes, counted off
`reports/title-label-proposals.tsv`, and each was ruled separately because they are
not the same question.

TWO GIVEN NAMES, ONE SURNAME -- 1,372 rows
    Mary|Maria Butler   Margaret|Anne Skipwith   Hans|Johann Greiffenclau zu Vollrads

    The FIRST is the label; the second becomes an `Amul`. Both given names are real
    usages, so both get a `P735` name item -- one name item per USAGE.

        Lmul  Mary Butler
        Amul  Maria Butler
        P735  Mary          P735  Maria          P734  Butler

THE PIPE INSIDE ONE NAME, NOTHING ANCHORING IT -- 136 rows
    Beatriz|Beatrice   Katherine|Catherine   Daniel Tichenor|Titchenal
    Dietrich Metzler|Seckel|Buettel

    THE SAME RULE, APPLIED AS IT FALLS. First token is the label, every later one an
    `Amul`. This is stated flatly because the consequence was put in front of the
    ruling and accepted: `Daniel Tichenor|Titchenal` yields the alias `Titchenal`,
    a bare surname with no given name on it.

A BRACKETED VARIANT GROUP -- 132 rows, and all 14 of its situations are ruled
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE BASE CASE -- 69 rows. The unbracketed name is the label; each bracketed spelling
becomes an `Amul` CARRYING THE GIVEN NAME. The parentheses never reach a label.

    Ann Bincks (Benckes|Bench)

        Label:  Ann Bincks
        Alias:  Ann Benckes
        Alias:  Ann Bench

The other thirteen situations were enumerated off the file and ruled ONE AT A TIME,
because they are not the same question and guessing any of them would have been a
positional parse.

A. A COMMA PHRASE AFTER THE BRACKET -- 34 rows.
   `Isabel Fraunceys (Francis|Frauncis), Heiress of Giffords Hall`
   KEEP IT IN THE `en` LABEL, NOT IN `mul`.

        Lmul   Isabel Fraunceys
        Len    Isabel Fraunceys, Heiress of Giffords Hall

   THE COMMA IS THE TELL. An un-comma'd `of X` is part of the name and stays -- see H1
   and H2 below, which were ruled the other way on exactly that difference.

B. A PIPE OUTSIDE THE BRACKET AS WELL AS INSIDE -- 10 rows. CROSS PRODUCT: every given
   name against every surname spelling.

        Judith|Godith Bosom (Bozon|Bosun)

        Label:  Judith Bosom
        Alias:  Judith Bozon      Alias:  Godith Bosom
        Alias:  Judith Bosun      Alias:  Godith Bozon
                                  Alias:  Godith Bosun

C. THE BRACKET IS THE WHOLE NAME -- 5 rows. No surname anywhere. First is the label, the
   rest are aliases. `(Thorkild|Tyrgils)` -> `Thorkild`, alias `Tyrgils`.

D. THE BRACKET IS ON THE GIVEN NAME -- 5 rows. The same rule, mirrored.
   `(Maria|Antiza) Golescu` -> `Maria Golescu`, alias `Antiza Golescu`.
   `Purcirto (Artinide|Hartneid) de Attems` -> `Purcirto Artinide de Attems`,
   alias `Purcirto Hartneid de Attems`.

E. `NN` AS THE GIVEN NAME -- 5 rows. NO NEW RULE: the NN/Private label algorithm already
   covers it -- `docs/rules/names.md`, THE NN/PRIVATE LABEL ALGORITHM APPLIES TO EVERY
   UNNAMED PERSON. `mul` is the marker plus the surname and the variants are `Amul`
   carrying the marker; the other languages take the FORMULAIC DESCRIPTIVE label built
   from the nearest named relative, owned by `scripts/build-nn-label-batch.py`, and never
   the bracket.

        NN de Haselrick (Hazelrigg|Hesilrige)

        Lmul   NN de Haselrick
        Amul   NN Hazelrigg      Amul   NN Hesilrige
        Len    <descriptive, from build-nn-label-batch.py>

   `NN (Mary|Elizabeth) Verney` is NOT this case: a given name IS recorded, twice, so it is
   situation D and the `NN` drops. -> `Mary Verney`, alias `Elizabeth Verney`.

F. THREE OR MORE VARIANTS -- 14 rows. One alias each, NO LIMIT.
   `Sarah Kilkam (Killem|Killum|Killam)` -> label plus three aliases.

G. A NEGATION IN THE BRACKET -- 1 row.
   `Margaret (not Cecily) Francis (Frauncis|Franceys|Frauncys)`
   `(not Cecily)` is a DESCRIPTION MARKER and comes out of the label; the spelling bracket
   behaves normally. Cecily is not emitted anywhere, as an alias or otherwise.

H1. AN EMPTY VARIANT SLOT -- 1 row, ruled on its own.
    `Kone (|Kunigunda) of (Hastevere)` -> `Kone of Hastevere`, alias `Kunigunda of
    Hastevere`. The empty slot is ignored and the second bracket is read as part of the name.

H2. SQUARE BRACKETS -- 1 row, ruled on its own.
    `(Teppana|Tahvana) of [Husu]` -> `Teppana of Husu`, alias `Tahvana of Husu`.
