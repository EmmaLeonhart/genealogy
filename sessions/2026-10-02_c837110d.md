**What the chain says** (NDL images 12–15):
- **Ame no Hiwashi** → his son **Ōasahiko** → Ōasahiko's son **Yufutsunushi**.
- Yufutsunushi marries **Iinagahime**, daughter of **Ame no Tomi**, who is Futodama's grandson. That ties the hemp clan's two divine lines together in one marriage.

**Why it looks invented:**
- After Katatanushi come 23 generations. Each is labelled with the palace of the next emperor in the official list, Annei through Kinmei, and almost none has a date.
- Some of those legendary emperors supposedly reigned for a century, yet each gets exactly one Inbe ancestor. The pedigree is laid over the emperor list rather than recording real people.
- Real death dates only start in the late 600s and 700s, such as Kumemaro, who died in 720.

**One possible link to a real court Inbe:** an 8th-century heir, Katsuyoshi, is said to be really the son of the court ritual official "Inbe no Sukune 色洞子". That is probably Inbe no Shikobuchi, who is in Wikidata. If so, it's the only place this pedigree touches a person from the chronicles, and it happens through an adoption. I haven't checked it yet.

I checked the official clan register of 815, the *Shinsen Shōjiroku*. It gives the hemp clan no chain of generations at all.

- **The hemp clan's entry is one line:** "descendants of Ame no Futodama, child of Takamimusubi." Many other clans in the same register give counts like "eighth-generation descendant of…", but the Inbe entry has no names and no number of generations.
- **The register gives Ame no Hiwashi three different ancestries.** Depending on the clan, he is Takamimusubi's grandson, or Kamimusubi's 5th-generation descendant, or his 7th.
- **Ame no Kushimimi isn't Futodama's son there.** The register makes him the son of a god called Ame no Kushitama. Wikidata's "Futodama → Kushimimi" only works if you treat Kushitama as another name for Futodama, and the Ono pedigree is the source that does that.
- **The Ono pedigree appears to be assembled from this register.** Its notes like "ancestor of the Hioki-be" and "ancestor of the Oyama-no-muraji" match register entries one for one. Its line from Kamimusubi to Hiwashi has exactly seven steps, matching the one entry that calls Hiwashi Kamimusubi's 7th-generation descendant. It looks like someone took the register's scattered counts and invented names to fill them in, which fits Kosugi's charge against Suzuki Masatoshi.

So the oldest official source names the hemp clan's divine ancestor and nothing in between. The chains only appear in 1870, and the most complete one looks pieced together from that register.
