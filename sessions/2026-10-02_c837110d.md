I'm taking "this clan" to mean the **Inbe (忌部, also written Imbe or 斎部)**, since that's the folder name. Tell me if you meant a different one.

You're right that they feel unreal, and there are concrete reasons for it:

- **They mostly survive in their own account.** A lot of what we know comes from the *Kogo Shūi* (807). Inbe no Hironari wrote it as a formal complaint to the emperor, protesting that the rival Nakatomi clan had squeezed the Inbe out of court ritual. So our main source is one side of a dispute.
- **Their "branches" may not be blood relatives.** A *-be* was originally a group organised around a job. The Inbe were the court's ritual-supply specialists: offering cloth, beads, spear shafts, timber, metal. The regional Inbe groups look less like a family tree and more like supply lines that were later given a shared mythical ancestry.
- **Their founder is a god.** Their ancestor is Futodama no Mikoto. In the cave myth, where Amaterasu hides and the world goes dark, he holds up the offerings while the Nakatomi ancestor, Ame-no-Koyane, recites the prayers. Their whole pedigree starts in myth.

The branches, as the *Kogo Shūi* lists them, each descend from a different god serving under Futodama:

| Branch | Region | Supposed ancestor | Specialty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central Inbe | Yamato (around today's Kashihara) | Futodama | Running the rituals at court |
| Awa Inbe | Awa province, today's Tokushima | Ame-no-Hiwashi | Hemp and bark cloth (*aratae*) |
| Sanuki Inbe | Kagawa | Taokiho-oi | Spear shafts |
| Kii Inbe | Wakayama | Hikosashiri | Timber, building palaces and shrines |
| Izumo Inbe | Shimane | Kushiakarutama | Jewels and *magatama* beads |
| Tsukushi and Ise Inbe | Kyushu and Mie | Ama-no-Mahitotsu | Metalwork and blades |

Two more things worth knowing:

- **Awa province in Chiba is named after them.** In their story, the Awa Inbe sailed east from Shikoku and named their new home after the old one. Awa Shrine in Tateyama still enshrines Futodama.
- **The line still has a living role.** The Miki family of Tokushima, traditionally Awa Inbe descendants, still weave the *aratae* hemp cloth for the enthronement ceremony. They did it for the current emperor in 2019.
