# The Inbe (忌部 / 斎部) clan: what is known

## The question (as the user put it)

Who were the Inbe, where did they come from, how did they spread, and can
their people be traced back, as a GEDCOM genealogy, to the gods, via Wikidata
or FamilySearch? Why do they feel so much less "real" than the Nakatomi, whose
genealogy is well defined? See `INTENT.md` for the user's own words.

## Current answer (early, 2026-10-02)

**Wikidata cannot get a real Inbe to the gods through Futodama.** It has the
god layer (Futodama, his father Takamimusubi, his children and grandson Ame no
Tomi) and about a dozen historical Inbe, but none of the historical people has
a parent. The two layers never meet. Confidence: high for what Wikidata
contains as of today.

**The branch ancestors are on different divine lines.** Even in the myth layer,
Ame no Hiwashi (Awa) descends from Takamimusubi via Omoikane, and Ama no
Mahitotsu (Tsukushi/Ise) descends from Susanoo. So "not all blood relatives"
holds in the myths themselves, not only in history. Confidence: medium (Wikidata
parentage, to check against the Kogo Shūi and Shinsen Shōjiroku).

**The one Wikidata path to the gods goes through the Oda's claimed Taira
descent**, not through the Inbe. Chikazane, an Inbe called the Oda ancestor, is
given the Taira as his father's line. Details are in
`notes/wikidata-survey.md`.

**FamilySearch has a 14-generation Inbe line, but it stops short of the gods.**
It runs Hamanari → Hironari → … → Fumiyama (822–867) → … → Chikazane (d. 1260),
and Hamanari has no parents. No link above Chikazane cites a source, and the
middle generations are too long. Details are in `notes/familysearch-survey.md`.

**Three god-to-family pedigrees exist, all from 1870 and all judged doubtful.**
The Awa Shrine (Takayama), Okajima and Ono (斎部宿禰本系帳) pedigrees are bound
in one public-domain NDL volume (pid 2538213). Each runs from Ame no Hiwashi
and Ame no Tomi (Futodama's grandson), through a marriage that founds the Awa
Inbe, then gives one ancestor per legendary emperor down to the 8th century and
beyond. The Awa and Okajima versions share the same list of names but space it
differently: five generations fewer, and the same people 100+ years apart.
Kosugi Sugimura judged all three forged or doubtful in 1876; the Ono one he
attributed to the forger Suzuki Masatoshi. Confidence: high that these are the
chains that exist and that they are late constructions. Details:
`notes/inbe-pedigrees.md`.

**FamilySearch's 14-generation line comes from Nakata Norinobu**, a Meiji
genealogist and Suzuki Masatoshi's close ally, in his account of Nobunaga's
ancestry. It also contradicts the 9th-century chronicle entry on Fumiyama,
which calls him humbly born. Details: `notes/familysearch-survey.md`.

**The Inbe's own 807 account (Kogo Shūi) gives no chain either.** Futodama
is Takamimusubi's son, and the regional ancestors (Hiwashi, Taokiho-oi,
Hikosashiri, Kushiakarutama, Ama no Mahitotsu) are his *followers*, not kin.
Ame no Tomi is his grandson in Jimmu's time, and there is nothing after that, not even
for the author himself. Details: `notes/kogo-shui.md`.

**The official 815 register gives the Inbe no chain at all**, only
"descendants of Futodama, child of Takamimusubi". It gives Ame no Hiwashi three
different ancestries. The Ono pedigree's generations can be matched to the
register's scattered "Nth-generation descendant" claims: Hiwashi is put exactly seven steps from
Kamimusubi, as one entry says. That suggests the chain was assembled from the
register. Details: `notes/shinsen-shojiroku.md`.

**No early or independent chain links any real Inbe to Futodama.** Every
multi-generation Inbe pedigree found traces back to Suzuki Masatoshi and his
circle (1870s–1900s). Wikidata has
the gods down to Ame no Tomi. FamilySearch has people back to Hamanari. Nothing
bridges the two. Confidence: high for these two databases; the primary texts
and printed pedigrees are not yet checked.

**Why the Nakatomi differ:** the Nakatomi have a pedigree compiled in 906 on
imperial order, and the Fujiwara kept records for centuries afterwards. The
Inbe were a ritual-supply organisation tied to the Soga, lost ground after
645, and had no great house to keep records. The gap is about power and
record-keeping more than reality. Details: `notes/origin-theories.md`,
`notes/nakatomi-comparison.md`.

**The Oda as an Inbe branch:** plausible but undocumented. The earliest Oda
document (1393, Tsurugi Shrine) is signed "Fujiwara". Chikazane is first named
in 1641, as a Taira. The Inbe version comes from undated pedigrees held by the
Oda shrine families. The 1290 "Chikazane" gravestone actually reads 親尊.
Details: `notes/oda-branch.md`.
