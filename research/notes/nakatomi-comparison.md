# Why the Nakatomi have a real genealogy and the Inbe don't

Started 2026-10-02. The user's question: "the Nakatomi clan have a much more
like actually defined genealogy, which makes me a bit unsure about this, this
clan."

## The Nakatomi have a pedigree from 906

- The **大中臣本系帳 (Ōnakatomi honkeichō)**, usually called the 中臣氏系図, was
  compiled in **906 (Engi 6)** by Ōnakatomi no Yasunori and others **at
  imperial command**. Work on it began in 893 and drew on an 863 genealogical
  record and the clan's old documents (Kotobank 大中臣氏; NDL Search, 続群書類従
  大中臣氏系図). It survives through quotation as the "延喜本系" ("Engi main
  lineage") inside the 中臣氏系図 printed in the **群書類従** collection.
- The 大中臣氏系図 in the **続群書類従** collection carries the family from the Nara period into
  the early Kamakura period.
- The Fujiwara, who split off from the Nakatomi with Kamatari, were then
  recorded in the most detailed genealogies Japan has, culminating in the
  14th-century **Sonpi Bunmyaku**.
- *To verify:* how far back the Engi honkei's generations run before Kamatari,
  and whether its early, mythical part is any more trustworthy than the Inbe
  ones. A pedigree compiled in 906 is still a construction for its earliest
  stretch.

## The Inbe have nothing comparable

| | Nakatomi | Inbe |
|---|---|---|
| Earliest surviving pedigree | 906, official, compiled from clan records | 1870 family submissions, judged forged in 1876 |
| In the 807–815 sources | (Kogo Shūi is anti-Nakatomi) | Ancestor god only; no generations, even from their own author |
| After 645 | Kamatari → Fujiwara, the leading court house for centuries | Lost ground; Ise offerings restricted to the Nakatomi from 757 |
| Who kept the records | Fujiwara and Ōnakatomi houses at the centre of court | No great house; regional shrine families (Awa, Susaki) |
| Chronicle people | Many, with parentage | About a dozen named people, none with stated parents |

## A reading of the gap

*Inference:* the difference is mostly about **power and record-keeping**, not
about one clan being "more real".
- The Nakatomi/Fujiwara were at the centre of the state. They had both the
  motive and the means to keep a pedigree, and the court itself ordered one
  in 906.
- The Inbe were an organisation of ritual suppliers under a modest family
  (首 rank until 680). They were tied to the Soga and lost out after 645.
  By the time pedigrees became the currency of status, nobody was keeping
  theirs.
- When Inbe shrine families needed pedigrees in 1870, for the Meiji Jingikan
  inquiry, they had none and got them made. In at least one case Suzuki
  Masatoshi made it, out of the 815 register's fragments.

So the Inbe feel "unreal" because the documentation that makes a clan feel
real was never made for them, or never survived. The individuals in the
chronicles (Kobito, Shikobuchi, Hironari, Hamanari, Fumiyama) are as real as
any Nakatomi of their time. What's missing is anything connecting them.
