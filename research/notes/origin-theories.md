# What the Inbe actually were: origin theories

Started 2026-10-02. Main source so far: Kokugakuin University 『古事記』氏族
database, entry 忌部首 <https://kojiki.kokugakuin.ac.jp/ujizoku/imibenoobito/>,
which summarises the scholarship. *Still to read directly: Ueda Masaaki
「忌部の職能」 (1961), Inoue Tatsuo 『古代王権と宗教的部民』 (1980), Hirabayashi
Akihito 「忌部氏と古代クラ制」 (1985).*

## The mainstream view: an organisation, not a family

- The Inbe were a **be (部)**, a group organised around work for the court.
  The central family, **忌部首 Inbe no obito**, was a *tomo no miyatsuko* (伴造),
  "a clan that led the be engaged in royal ritual" (王権の祭祀に従事した部を
  統率した伴造氏族).
- The **regional Inbe** (Awa, Sanuki, Kii, Izumo, Tsukushi, …) were groups that
  produced ritual goods: cloth, spear shafts, timber, jewels, metal. They were
  not branches of one bloodline. The central Inbe controlled their workshops,
  for example the jewel-making at the **Soga site** (曽我遺跡, Kashihara, near
  the central Inbe's home ground).
- *Inference:* this is why the genealogy looks so strange. A shared ancestor,
  Futodama, holding a shared organisation together is what you would expect
  if "Inbe" named a job and a chain of command rather than a family. The Kogo
  Shūi says the same thing in mythic form: the regional ancestors are
  Futodama's *followers*, not his children (see `kogo-shui.md`).

## Rank history (kabane)

- **首 obito** at first, a modest tomo-no-miyatsuko rank.
- **連 muraji** in 680.
- **宿禰 sukune** from 684, under Tenmu's eight-kabane reform.
- The name changed from 忌部 to **斎部** in 803. The entry reads it as an attempt to
  restore standing while the clan was declining.

## Why the Inbe lost to the Nakatomi

- Before the 645 coup the two were roughly equal ritual families.
- The Inbe were **close to the Soga** (忌部首と親しい関係にあった蘇我大臣家).
  When the Soga main line was destroyed in 645 (the Isshi incident), led by
  Nakatomi no Kamatari, the Nakatomi's advantage "became clear".
- By 757 the envoys carrying offerings to Ise were restricted to the
  Nakatomi. The Inbe fought back with lawsuits in 806 and the Kogo Shūi in 807.
- *Inference, linking to the user's question:* the Nakatomi became the
  Fujiwara, the most powerful lineage in Japan, which kept meticulous
  pedigrees for centuries. The Inbe went the other way: declining after 645,
  out of central politics by the Heian period, and with no great house to keep
  their records. That may explain much of the gap in documentation. See
  `nakatomi-comparison.md`, still to write.
