# The regional Inbe branches

Started 2026-10-03. The Oda (Echizen) are in `oda-branch.md`. The 807 Kogo
Shūi's list of branches is in `kogo-shui.md`.

## Awa (Shikoku): the Miki family of Koyadaira

- **What is documented:** the Miki family of Koyadaira (木屋平, now Mima
  city, Tokushima) hold the **三木家文書, about 45 documents from the
  Kamakura to Muromachi periods**. These show them as **御殿人** (people
  attached to the court), hereditarily responsible for supplying **麁服
  aratae**, the hemp cloth for the Daijōsai enthronement rite. The documents are a Tokushima
  prefectural tangible cultural property, and the early-Edo house is a
  National Important Cultural Property (Mima city tourism site; ja Wikipedia
  三木家住宅).
- The supply stopped in the late Muromachi turmoil and was **revived after
  about 580 years for Emperor Taishō's enthronement** (1915). It continued
  for Shōwa, Heisei and, in 2019, Reiwa. Miki Nobuo supervised the 2019
  aratae (Nikkei, 2019).
- **Genealogy:** an NDL reference-service answer (crd.ndl.go.jp
  1000350493) treats the Miki as "the direct line of the Awa Inbe". The
  genealogies it cites are modern compilations: 『大嘗祭に阿波忌部奉仕の由来
  三木由緒』 (1994); Saitō Mitsuru 『阿波の忌部』 (1984); Kubo Tadao
  『麻植郡郷土誌』 (1917); Miki Hiroto 『木屋平村史』 (1971). It notes that
  the Awa Inbe had several family branches.
- *Inference:* this is the **strongest evidence of continuity anywhere in the
  Inbe story**. It is not a pedigree back to the gods. It is medieval
  documents showing one family doing the Awa Inbe's job, supplying aratae
  for the enthronement, as the Kogo Shūi says the Awa Inbe did in 807. It shows
  the *function* continued. Any genealogy linking the Miki to the ancient
  Inbe still needs checking. The cited ones date from 1917 onwards.
- *To do:* see whether the earliest 三木家文書 name the family as Inbe, or only
  as 御殿人; find their earliest date.
