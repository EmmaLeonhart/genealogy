# The junction hunt — where an unrecorded link to Yuri could actually be

The surname instrument is exhausted (see `yuri-descent-candidates.md`). This is what replaced it.

⛔ **THE TARGET IS EUPHEMIA, NOT YURI.** The point of descent from Yuri is his MOTHER's line,
which the existing Mstislav tie cannot give — Mstislav's mother is recorded `NN` in our tree and
he is a half-brother. So **a full sibling counts exactly as much as Yuri does**, and our tree
records five of them.

**Euphemia's whole issue is 168,902 people, of whom 16,537 are reachable only through a full
sibling and never through Yuri.** Every instrument below runs against the 168,902.

**The widening bought no new surnames.** Against Euphemia's issue the maternal side shares the
same twelve real surnames it shared with Yuri's alone. Widening the target did not open a surname
route, which is what sent the hunt to places instead.

**The Bure heartland is nearly dead ground.** The owner's maternal frontier clusters hard on
Skellefteå — Bureå, Burträsk, Storkåge, Gummark, Bjursiljum, Renbergsvattnet, Falmark, Yttervik —

⛔ **AND THE NORWEGIAN CONTACT IS THE WRONG SORT.** Of the 481 placed in Norway, **172 are
Oslo/Kristiania and 108 Bergen**, against 44 in Rogaland/Jæren where the owner's paternal frontier
sits; the median birth year is **1853**, so most of that contact is urban and a century too late
to matter. The junction hunt on the paternal side returns **zero** candidates against 468 placed
frontier people, and this is why.

## The junction instrument

A frontier ancestor — one whose parents our tree does not record — could be the child of one of
Euphemia's descendants. In Swedish data the patronymic *names the father outright*, so the
frontier person carries a direct statement about the parent we are missing. Screen: the father's
given name from the patronymic, a plausible 16–55 year gap, and **the same region**.

Unfiltered it returns 301 links, which is noise — `Johan` and `Erik` match half the Swedish
peerage. With the region filter applied, **the maternal side returns exactly one** and the
paternal side none. Full unfiltered output: `reports/yuri-junction-candidates.csv`.

### The one candidate

| | |
|---|---|
| **frontier ancestor** | `Lars Persson` **1752–1811**, Vingåker, Södermanland — Geni `6000000173443464858` |
| parents | **none recorded** |
| spouse | Maja Andersdotter, of Bromma *soldattorp*, V. Vingåker |
| the owner's line from him | Per Larsson 1785 → Carl Johan Persson 1815 → Hedvig Christina Carlsdotter 1846 → Beda Elvira Wedberg 1906 → Hans Bertil Frisk 1930 → Helen Frisk → owner |
| **possible father** | `Per Ribbing` **1717–1802**, Årdala, Södermanland; died Ekeby, Östergötland — Geni `6000000218618194333` |
| his issue in our tree | **none recorded — no children, no spouse** |
| his line | Ribbing af Zernava, back through Lennart 1676 → Leonard 1638 → Peder Lindormsson 1606 → Lindorm 1569 → Peder Svensson 1544 → Sven Knutsson 1499 → Knut Pedersson 1458 → Peder Knutsson 1409 |

**What it is:** a parentless boy in a Södermanland soldier-croft family whose patronymic says his
father was a Per, and a Södermanland nobleman of the right age carrying Euphemia's blood who our
records give no children at all. Årdala to Vingåker is about 50 km. Ribbing is independently one
of the larger Euphemia-descended families on Swedish soil — 50 people, 1670–1882, concentrated in
Stockholm and Södermanland.

**What it is not:** evidence. `Per` is among the commonest Swedish given names, the instrument
matched on name, region and arithmetic and on nothing else, and the social gap means the tree
would record nothing either way. **The decisive document is the Vingåker parish birth register for
1752**, which names the father or marks the birth *oäkta* — that is the next step, and it is not
in our data.

⛔ **AND THE FUNNEL IS NARROW ENOUGH TO STATE.** 222 maternal frontier people → 171 locatable →
**79** with both a place and a date → those with a parseable patronymic → one survivor. The
instrument is weakest exactly where the tree is thinnest, so *one candidate* means one candidate
**among the people we can place**, not one in the ancestry.

## The target population, mapped — `reports/yuri-in-scandinavia.csv`

5,447 Yuri-descended people carry a birth or death place in Sweden, Finland, Estonia, Livonia or
Latvia. By half-century of birth: 7 before 1550, 18 to 1599, 98 to 1649, 405 to 1699, **948 to
1749, 1,520 to 1799, 1,314 to 1849**, 891 to 1899.

**The first arrivals are Baltic, not Swedish, and they are early.** Alexander Lüneburg, born Reval
1240; Johann von Lüneburg II, died Reval 1325. Then **a 175-year silence** — nothing 1350–1499 —
before the first Swedish-soil appearance, Ilsebe von Uexküll, born Skokloster, Uppland, **1500**.
From there the Baltic German houses (Uexküll, Wrangell, Tiesenhausen, Rosen, Taube, Vietinghoff,
Dönhoff) and the Vasa line appear together through the 16th century and the geography is populated
continuously from about 1600.

**The largest families:** Stackelberg 133, Sparre 120, Wrangel 75, Falkenberg 71, Toll 67,
Klingspor 65, Uggla 60, Posse 58, Rosen 53, Mörner 52, **Ribbing 50**, Taube 49, Lewenhaupt 47,
Liewen 46, Horn 39, Essen 38, Bonde 37, Fock 37. About half are Baltic German houses holding
Estonian and Livonian estates; the rest are native Swedish Riddarhuset families. Family grouping
there is a heuristic off the label string, not a verified house.

## Bure is closed, and it closed negative

The two Bure patrilines our tree holds apart — the owner's `Herse Andersson Bure` (d. 1549) and the
`Sven Pedersson` (~1475) line that carries the Rurikid blood from 1713 — **do not join, and this is
refuted rather than merely unrecorded.**

- Our tree: Line A tops out at an **unnamed man born 1310×1370**; Line B tops out at
  **Sven Pedersson**, father and mother blank. **No shared person anywhere**, patriline or maternal
  branches.
- Wikidata holds less, not more: `Q121204047` *Laurentius Svenonis* carries children but **no
  `P22`/`P25` at all**, and Line A's people return nothing under their own names.
- The external evidence is decisive. Johan Bure's own 17th-century manuscript marks this branch
  with a double cross meaning *probably no connection, added afterward on insufficient grounds*;
  Sikeborg's review of the manuscript (rediscovered 2008) finds the branch absent from the
  original; and the 2013 Sjölund & Norberg Y-DNA study has Line A's descendants at **G2A
  (G-Y12970)** against **R1b** for the claimed branch. The three ennobled Bure brothers, sons of
  Engelbertus Laurentii, hold their real Bure connection **through their mother Elisabeth
  Andersdotter** and not through the male line at all.

So the Rurikid blood in the Bure name sits on a patriline that is not the owner's, and the document
trail that once joined them is a known error. *(Sourcing here is a research pass over sv.wikipedia
`Bureätten` and the cited study, not a primary reading; the tree and Wikidata halves are measured.)*

## Where this leaves it

1. **Södermanland, 1650–1800** is the only maternal zone with both populations present in numbers.
   `Lars Persson` 1752 is the single scored candidate and it needs a parish register.
2. **Västerbotten is close to ruled out** on contact alone — 52 people.
3. **Norway is ruled out for the relevant period** — the contact is urban and median 1853.
4. **Surnames are done**, against Yuri and against Euphemia's whole issue alike.
5. **The frontier is the bottleneck, not the method.** Only 79 of 222 maternal frontier people
   carry both a place and a date. Anything that thickens the frontier — the descendant reports
   parsed into structure, above all — widens this instrument directly.
