# Samaritan family genealogy: what the sources actually name

**Everything here is from published sources, transcribed 2026-08-13.** None of it
is in the corpus yet. It is written down because it is the material that was
missing when `reports/samaritan-component.md` concluded the 33 could not be
joined — and several of these people are named ancestors with centuries attached,
which is exactly what that report said did not exist.

The single richest source is the **Israelite Samaritan Information Institute's
families page**, written by Benyamim Tsedaka. The second is *The Tsedaka Family*,
a 2008 article from the library of the editor of theSamaritanUpdate.com.

---

## House of the High Priests — tribe of Levi

> "Priests of the **Aabtaa** (Arabic *Dar Elhaftawi*) are descended from
> **Itamar ben Aaron**, priest and brother of Moses."

So `ha'Åbtå'i` = Aabtaa = Dar Elhaftawi. This is the family our disconnected
component belongs to.

**The 1787 bottleneck, and it is a hard genealogical fact rather than colour:**

> "only one 4-year-old child survived after his father died in 1787. Nevertheless,
> the line recovered, and all the present family are descendants of this child,
> **Shalma ben Tabia**."

**Every living member of the priestly family descends from Shalma ben Tabia.**
That is a single point of descent, and Shalma ben Tabia *is in our component*
(`Shalma II ben Tabia`, `6000000178794831823`).

**Three sub-families of the priestly house:**

| sub-family | Arabic | eponym |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House of Pinchas | *Dar Chader* | Pinchas ben Issak |
| House of Issak | *Dar Aschak* | Issak ben Amram |
| House of Jacob | *Dar Yaaqub* | Jacob ben Aaron |

**Yakov b. Aharon, High Priest 1896–1916** — in our component as
`Yaacob I ben Aaharon ben Shalma` `6000000178795028823`.

## House of Tsedaka Hassafari — tribe of Menasseh

**Founder: `Safar ben Jacob`, lived in the 14th century CE.** That is the head of
the family, six centuries deep, named.

**The renaming, early 20th century:**

> "the family of **Abraham ben Marchiv Hassafari** moved to Jaffa. There, they
> adopted a new family name, *Tsedaka*, after **Tsedaka ben Ab-Za'uta**, an 18th
> century forefather."

**Sub-branches:** Marchiv ben Jacob (*Dar Farag*) · Ubab (*Dar Chabib*)

**An extinct branch with a death date:** the family of **Arieh ben Shalma**
(*Dar Elnimir*) survived until its last member died **25 March 2012**. The
Tsedaka-family article gives him as **Arieh [Nimr] = Yisaschar ben Shalma**, a
cousin of Abraham ben Marchiv's father, who moved to Tul Karm; his family lived
there nearly two generations without taking root.

**Yefet ben Abraham** established the Samaritan neighbourhood in Holon and served
as head of the Samaritans outside Shechem **until his death in 1982**.

**The literary line, three named men across two centuries:** Abraham ben Marchiv
(19th c.), **Ratson ben Benyamim** and **Yisrael ben Gamliel** (20th c.).

### The Tsedaka chain as it now stands

From the institute's page on Ratson, the families page, and the 2008 article:

**`Batia bat Yefet ben Abraham` is a named woman with a named father.** She is
the only one found in this entire search, and she marries the two Tsedaka lines
together.

## Dinfi family — tribe of Ephraim

> "One small family escaped from Damascus to Shechem in **1625**, thus avoiding a
> pogrom which destroyed the remnants of the Samaritan community there."

**Sirrawi branch** — founder **Yishmael ben Ab-Sikuwwa**, who "served as a
secretary in the Shechem administration (Arabic: *Kateb Sirri*)", and gave his
name to the oldest branch, *Dar Sirrawi*.

**Altif branch — this is the origin of the Altif name:**

> "**Abed Hanuna ben Jacob Hadinfi**, nicknamed *Iltafe*, or 'handsome'. So the
> name of the family branch became **Altif** (*Dar Iltafe*)."

So Altif is a **branch of the Dinfi family**, descended from Abed Hanuna ben
Jacob Hadinfi — and the Ukrainian marriages of the 2000s (Alexandra Kraskuk →
Wadah Altif; Alla Evdokimova → Azzam Altif) attach to that branch.

**Extinct Dinfi branches:** *Dar Imsallam*, after **Amshallemaa ben
Ab-Sikkuwwa** — so a brother of Yishmael — and *Dar Elshalabi*, after **Sadaqa
ben Jacob**.

**19th century:** **Ta'or ben Jacob**.

## Marchiv family — tribe of Ephraim

Founder lived in the **14th century**. Split in the 18th century into two
families:

| family | Arabic | eponym |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marchiv | *Dar Mfarreg* | Marchiv ben Abraham |
| Yehoshua | *Dar Aosh'a* | Yehoshua, **brother of Marchiv ben Abraham** |

**Named historical figures:** Marchiv ben Jacob ben Yusef (17th c.), Yusef ben
Yehoshua (18th c.), Abraham ben Yashishakar.

## Tribe of Benyamin — extinct 1968

Family name **Matar** (*Dar Elmatari*), arrived from Gaza to Shechem in the 18th
century. The last member, **a woman, died in 1968** — unnamed in the source.

**Tabia ben Ab-Za'uta**, "a great poet and commentator who served as governor of
Jaffa in the 18th century."

**Note the patronymic.** Tabia ben Ab-Za'uta and **Tsedaka ben Ab-Za'uta** — the
18th-century forefather the Tsedaka family renamed itself after — share a father,
across what the page presents as two different tribes. That is either a cross-family
link worth chasing or a repeated name, and the page does not say which.

## What this changes

`reports/samaritan-component.md` concluded that nothing above the component was
named. **That is now wrong in one specific and important way.** The priestly
family's own account gives:

- **Shalma ben Tabia** as the single ancestor of every living member of the
  priestly family, dated by his father's death in **1787** — and he is already in
  our component.
- The **Aabtaa/Dar Elhaftawi** identification of the family, and its claimed
  descent from **Itamar ben Aaron**.
- Three named sub-family eponyms below him.

What is still missing is what was missing before: **a father for Tabia
ha'Åbtå'i**, and the stretch between him and Itamar. The 1787 bottleneck is
*below* our component's root, not above it, so it does not help the join — but it
does mean the family's structure is documented in a way the earlier report said
it was not.

The Tsedaka, Dinfi, Marchiv and Matar material is a different matter: **none of
those people are in the corpus at all**, and they are named, dated, and organised
into branches. They are import material rather than connection material.
