# What the 2026-08-05 batch changed

Forty zips arrived; 37 distinct GEDCOMs went into the merge. This report is the
before/after, and the per-export contribution that the aggregate numbers hide.

**Where the files are.** `exports/fleshing-out/export-geni/*.ged`, on disk and
deliberately out of git (~200 MB). The synoptic GEDCOM is regenerated with:

```bash
python -m genimerge merge data_lake/*.ged exports/fleshing-out/export-geni/*.ged
```

which writes `out/merged.ged` — 262 MB, gitignored like everything in `out/`.

### What that says

**Every one of the 37 added something.** The lowest marginal is 29 and there are
no zeros, so nothing in the batch was wasted, even though several exports were
taken minutes apart from neighbouring seeds.

**The spread is enormous: 29 to 3985.** Median 2282, mean 2194. So an export is
worth somewhere between 0.7% and 99.5% of its own size, and the size of the file
tells you almost nothing about what it brings — 30 of the 37 hit the 4004/4008
ceiling and their marginal ranges across nearly that whole span.

**Taking a second export minutes after the first, near the same place, is where
the waste is.** `export-Forest-9` holds 3187 people the old tree lacked but only
**35** the batch lacked, because `export-Forest-8` was taken two minutes earlier
and covered the rest. `export-Forest-38` is the same shape: 727 against 29.

**Batch-internal redundancy is 14.7%** — the exports collectively hold 95,177
people new to the old tree, but only 81,202 distinct ones. That is the price of
taking many exports quickly without checking between them, and it is lower than
it might have been.

**The best single export, `export-Forest-15`, was 99.5% new** — 3985 of its 4004
people had never been seen. Whatever seeded it was in genuinely fresh territory.

The thin share barely moved — 59.3% to 60.0%. Adding 37 exports made the tree
much bigger without making it proportionally better covered, which is what you
would expect from exports that reach outward rather than fill in. The largest
thin region is now a different neighbourhood entirely (seed Guillaume de
Chauvigny) and is **half again the size** of the previous largest.

## Errors grew faster than the tree, again

| | before | after | growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| people | 105,349 | 186,551 | ×1.77 |
| impossible dates | 1,548 | 2,746 | ×1.77 |
| implausible dates | 916 | 1,761 | ×1.92 |
| likely duplicate profiles | 27 | 99 | ×3.67 |
| possible duplicates | 490 | 1,476 | ×3.01 |

**The impossible-date rate held exactly flat this time** at ×1.77, which is new —
the standing `queue.md` concern was that errors grew faster than the tree (10→54
exports grew people ×3.9 and impossible dates ×5.9). Over this batch they grew
in step. That is one data point against "the checks scale badly with tree size"
and for "the earlier material was worse", but it does not settle it.

**Duplicate detection did not hold flat** — ×3.67 and ×3.01 against ×1.77
people. Duplicate-finding compares people against each other, so its candidate
pool grows faster than linearly with tree size; this is the expected shape and
not yet evidence of anything about the data.

## What this report does not say

Presence and contribution measure **our sampling**, never Geni's content. An
export with a low marginal count did not fail — it landed somewhere already
covered. And nothing here classifies anyone geographically; a region is a
neighbourhood in the family graph.
