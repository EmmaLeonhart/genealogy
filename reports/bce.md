# BCE in a corpus that cannot express it

`out/merged.ged` contains **zero `BC` strings**. Every Geni export in this repo
writes BCE years as bare positives, so a person who lived in 2111 BCE is recorded
as born in 2111 and is indistinguishable from a CE person by reading the number.

Generated by `scripts/find-bce.py`, offline, over the 151-export tree.

## What is certain

**Five people carry a birth year later than 2026**, which no living or dead
person can, so their era marker is definitely missing:

| year | who |
| ---: | --- |
| 2216 | Merenre Nemtyemsaf II, Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2191 | Hetep |
| 2166 | Sehertawy Intef I "the Great", Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2111 | Nebhepetre Mentuhotep II, Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2060 | Sesostris |

Mentuhotep II reads birth 2111, death 2046 — birth *after* death, which is what
a BCE lifespan looks like with the era gone.

**Everyone above them is BCE too, whatever their record says.** Walking up from
those five gives **176 ancestors**, and that needs no inference: an ancestor of a
Middle Kingdom pharaoh did not live in the common era. So **181 people are
certainly BCE**, as a floor.

## The part that matters, and it is small

**Of those 181, only 7 carry a birth date at all** — the five above, plus two of
the 176 ancestors (years 2111 and 2216). The other 174 are undated.

That is the answer to the question the century report raised. `reports/centuries.md`
counts only *dated* people, so the confirmed BCE contamination of its Geni column
is **7 of 147,984 — 0.005%**, and five of those seven are visible as impossible
years rather than hidden. The distribution is not meaningfully distorted.

The reason is structural rather than lucky: this corpus's BCE population is
ancient and legendary, and those profiles rarely carry dates on Geni. The
`BCE | Geni 0` row in `reports/centuries.md` is still an artifact — we hold
pharaohs — but it is an artifact covering seven dated people, not thousands.

## Two weaker signals, reported as weak

**Birth year after death year: 74 people.** Twelve of those have a death year
under 100 against a birth year over 100 — a truncated date (`2 DATE 12`, a day
with no year), not BCE. The remaining **62** are BCE-shaped. They are not
confirmed BCE: an ordinary transposition produces the same shape.

**Parent born after child: 963 pairs, 1,650 people.** BCE inverts this direction,
so a BCE lineage shows up here — but so does any wrong year. Treat it as an upper
bound on the disorder rather than a count of BCE people; nothing here separates
the two, and pretending otherwise would turn a signal into a false population.

## What this is for

**The century report is fine.** 0.005% confirmed contamination changes no
conclusion in it.

**The authoring pipeline is not.** `out/wikidata/add-claims.qs` builds P569/P570
from these dates. Left alone it would state on Wikidata that Nebhepetre
Mentuhotep II was **born in 2111 CE**, sourced to a Geni profile. Seven dated
people is a small enough number to handle by hand and a bad enough error to be
worth handling before the batch runs.

Nothing here corrects a date. What the corpus should hold — whether the era
markers are recoverable from Geni, whether the merged GEDCOM should carry
negative years, or whether the pipeline should simply refuse to emit a date for
a flagged person — is Emma's call.
