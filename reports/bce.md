# BCE dates: the corpus handles them, five records do not

**This report previously claimed the corpus could not express BCE at all. That
was wrong, and it was my bug, not the data's.** The claim rested on
`out/merged.ged` containing zero `BC` strings — true, and irrelevant. Geni writes
BC years as a **minus**: `-73`, `ABT -95`, `BEF -1310`. There are **4,750** such
`DATE` lines. The parser I had hand-rolled used `str.isdigit()`, which is `False`
for `"-73"`, so it dropped every one of them in silence and I read that silence
as absence.

`genimerge.dates` has parsed these correctly since 2026-08-05, and its docstring
records the same bug being found and fixed once before — 4,459 events reduced to
`year=None`, invisible because an unreadable date is discarded by design. Both
`scripts/find-bce.py` and `scripts/build-centuries.py` now call `parse_date`.

Regenerated by `scripts/find-bce.py`, offline, over the 151-export tree.

## What is actually true

**The tree holds 2,256 BCE people with dates**, 1.5% of its dated population,
against Wikidata's 404. Ancient and legendary lines are a place where our tree is
richer than the other side, not poorer.

**Five records have the sign missing.** All Egyptian pharaohs, all carrying a
positive birth year later than 2026, which no real person can:

| year as recorded | who |
| ---: | --- |
| 2216 | Merenre Nemtyemsaf II, Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2191 | Hetep |
| 2166 | Sehertawy Intef I "the Great", Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2111 | Nebhepetre Mentuhotep II, Pharaoh of Egypt |
| 2060 | Sesostris |

Mentuhotep II reads birth 2111, death 2046 — birth after death, which is what a
BCE pair looks like once the minus is gone. He reigned c. 2061–2010 BCE.

**Their ancestors are fine.** Walking up from those five reaches 176 people, of
whom 42 carry dates — and those dates run from **-3305 to 2216**, i.e. correctly
negative. So the missing sign is confined to a handful of records rather than
propagating through a lineage. Five errors, not a broken convention.

## The weaker signals, now that they mean something different

**Birth year after death year: 67 people**, of which 16 are a truncated date
(`2 DATE 12`, a day with no year). The remaining **51** are the shape a missing
minus makes — five are confirmed above, the rest are unexplained and may equally
be ordinary transpositions.

**Parent born after child: 1,018 pairs, 1,752 people.** A missing minus inverts
this, and so does any wrong year. An upper bound on disorder, not a count of
anything.

Both counts moved when the parser was fixed (from 74 and 963), because dates that
had been invisible became comparable.

## What to do about it

**The century report needed no rescue** — `reports/centuries.md` now shows the
2,256 correctly, and its conclusions were about the 1700s–1900s where none of
this reaches.

**The authoring pipeline still needs the five.** `out/wikidata/add-claims.qs`
builds P569/P570 from these dates, so left alone it would state on Wikidata that
Nebhepetre Mentuhotep II was **born in 2111 CE**, sourced to a Geni profile.
Five records is small enough to correct by hand.

Correcting them means editing the profiles on Geni, since this repo does not
change export data — **BLOCKED-ON-USER-ACTION**, Emma. The alternative, if that
is not worth doing for five pharaohs, is for the pipeline to refuse a date for
any person whose birth year exceeds the current year, which is a two-line guard
and catches the whole class rather than these five.
