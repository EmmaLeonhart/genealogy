# The blood line to Charlemagne, and where it thins

Emma, 2026-08-13: *"do this link to Charlemagne instead of the other one.
Ancestry is good like this. I want to see which points where the descendants
clearly thin in a way that suggests I'll need another export."*

Geni's own **shortest blood relationship** — Charlemagne as 35th great
grandfather, **37 steps, every one a parent**. This supersedes
`reports/charlemagne-route.md`, which minimised people-to-create and got a
398-step route through spouses and children: cheaper, and not a lineage.

**35 of the 38 people are already in the merged tree**, and
the line connects end to end apart from 1 broken edge and
3 absent people.

## Where the line breaks

- **step 4 → 5**: *Rakel Rasmusdottir Borsheim* → *Anne Berta Osmundsdatter Nese*. Both are in the tree; the parent link is not. Geni calls this relation *"adoptive mother"* — **an adoption**, and our derived family edges carry birth parents only. The tree gives Rakel Rasmusdottir Borsheim's parents as someone else entirely.
- **step 13**: *Katarina Galte* — absent. Its neighbours are held, so this is a single missing person rather than a missing stretch.
- **step 24**: *Torbjørg Åsulvsdatter Austrått, til Rein* — absent. Its neighbours are held, so this is a single missing person rather than a missing stretch.
- **step 25**: *Lendmann Åsulf Guttormsson på Rein* — absent. Its neighbours are held, so this is a single missing person rather than a missing stretch.

**None of these breaks needs an export to fix.** Two are people Geni has and
we did not sample; one is an adoption edge that the GEDCOM records with a
pedigree tag our family derivation drops.

## Where the descendants thin — the export answer

Descendant counts rise monotonically up a lineage: every ancestor's
descendants include everyone below them. So the count is not the signal. The
**growth from one step to the next** is: an ancestor who adds one person is
one we hold *only* as a link in this chain, with every sibling of the next
step unexplored.

**14 of the 34 steps add two people or fewer.** They are not
scattered — they come in runs, and a run is what a single export fixes.

## Reading it

- **Steps 16–23, roughly 1430 back to 1200** — the Måge / Aga / Galtung /
  Galte stretch — is the thin run that matters. Eight consecutive
  generations, each with **exactly one recorded child**, the whole run adding
  seven people. We have a bare thread through two and a half centuries of
  Norwegian ancestry and nothing on either side of it.
- **Steps 9–11, 1697 back to 1625** is the same shape, shorter and later.
- **Everything above step 26 is dense** — Guttorm à Rein carries 10,070
  descendants and Judith of Flanders 24,874. That end of the line is
  well-sampled and needs nothing.
- **The Wikidata column turns on at the same place.** Steps 15–23 have four
  Wikidata-linked descendants between them; step 26 has 585 and step 29 has
  1,623. The thin run is invisible to Wikidata as well as to us.

**So one export, seeded in the 1200–1430 stretch, is the pick.** A `Forest`
or `Descendants` take from any of steps 16–23 would fan out across siblings
this line has never touched.

## Caveats

- **Names resolved without profile IDs.** The screenshots carry no `href`s, so
  every person here was found by name against every rendering the repo holds.
  Saving the page and running `path-from-html` would make this exact.
- **Descendant counts measure our exports, never Geni.** A thin ancestor is one
  *we* barely sampled. Whether Geni holds more there is what an export settles.
- **Step 5 is an adoption** and Geni still calls the path a blood relationship.
  Recorded as it stands rather than reinterpreted.
