# The Ukrainian marriages into the Samaritan community

**Emma's line of enquiry, 2026-08-14, and she was ahead of it throughout.** She
named Yousef Cohen as the thread to pull, asked for the Ukrainian marriages
specifically, and when four pages returned 403 she **downloaded all four
herself**. Those saved pages are what settled the surname question and corrected
two of my claims — the Altif attribution and the Elazar-as-father reading. This
file is the write-up of a search she directed, not a discovery.

Press research only — none of this is in Geni or in the local Wikidata store, and
the point of writing it down is that these are the **only contemporary Samaritan
wives anywhere on record by name**.

`reports/samaritan-marriages.md` established the hole: 99 family records in the
priestly tree, two wives, both ancient. These women fill part of it.

---

## The rule changes

| year | change |
| ---: | --- |
| 1973 | the High Priest permits marriage to **Jewish** women |
| 2002 | permitted to **Ukrainian** women |
| 2003 | the first Ukrainian woman arrives |

Every match needs the **High Priest's approval**, and the bride serves a trial
period of up to a year in the community before the religious ceremony. Matches
came through a Tel Aviv marriage agency and Ukrainian agencies run by Ukrainian
Jews, working from printed catalogues. About **10–11** Ukrainian women had come
by 2013–14; brides have also arrived from **Kazakhstan, Turkey and Russia**.

The driver is the sex ratio: **men outnumber women roughly three to one**.

## SETTLED: he is Wadah **Cohen**, and the high priest was his **uncle**

**RFE/RL, read from the page Emma saved 2026-08-14, is unambiguous** — and it
overturns both the AP surname and my own reading of the Globe and Mail:

> "**Alexandra Cohen (formerly Krasuk)** and her two children in the village of
> Mount Gerizim."

> "In 2003, Krasuk was studying management in Kherson when businessman **Wadah
> Cohen** spotted a photograph of the petite blonde in a dating agency catalog."

> "Cohen found himself single at 40. **Two of his brothers are deaf mutes**,
> which he said was the result of inbreeding. He told **his uncle, the late High
> Priest Shalom Ben-Amram Cohen**, that marrying Krasuk would end his solitude
> and widen the gene pool — and got his blessing."

It also says "seven years after **the Cohens'** marriage". So:

- **The surname is Cohen.** The Times of Israel / Washington Examiner / Blaze
  "Wadah Altif" and "Shura Altif" are wrong, and `reports/samaritan-families.md`
  inherited that error.
- **The relationship is uncle, not father**, and the high priest is a different
  man from the one I named. The Globe and Mail (2007) has "the genial,
  white-bearded cleric… helped **his son** pick out… Alexandra" and identifies
  him as High Priest **Elazar Ben Tzadaka**. **Prefer RFE/RL**: the marriage was
  2003, and the sitting High Priest in 2003 was **Shalom ben Amram** (2001–2004),
  not Elazar ben Tsedaka (2004–2010). The Globe and Mail, writing in 2007, looks
  to have attached the story to whoever was high priest when it published.
- My "the son is named Eliazar after his grandfather Elazar" inference is
  therefore **dead**. It was a reasonable pattern and it was wrong.

### And the uncle is a profile we already hold

**High Priest Shalom Ben-Amram Cohen is `Saloum /Cohen/`** — Geni
`6000000178793768878`, Wikidata **`Q2067443`**, **b. 1921 d. 2004**, the 18th
Itamar-line High Priest (**2001–2004**), whose father in our data is `Amram ben
Yitzhaq`. That matches the succession list's "Shalom b. 'Amram b. Yitzhaq b.
'Amram" exactly, and the death year matches the end of his term.

**So Wadah Cohen's father is a missing son of Amram ben Yitzhaq.** Geni records
`Amram ben Yitzhaq` (`6000000178795370821`, `Q135489965`) as having **exactly one
child — Saloum**. If Wadah is Saloum's nephew, Wadah's father is Saloum's brother
and is absent from Geni altogether, along with Wadah and his three children.

This is the first place in the whole exercise where a named living family
attaches to a specific missing node in our tree.

## The earlier reading, kept because it was wrong in a useful way

**Two reputable sources give her husband two different surnames.**

> "With his son, the genial, white-bearded cleric went through a catalogue sent
> by the Ukrainian marriage agency and helped his son pick out the slim and
> pretty Alexandra, hoping she would bear him healthy grandchildren."
> — The Globe and Mail, 2007

That piece names the cleric as **High Priest Elazar Ben Tzadaka** and the son as
**Wada Cohen**, 45, with Alexandra becoming **"Ms. Cohen"**. The AP-derived
coverage of 2013 (Times of Israel, Washington Examiner, Blaze) instead calls him
**Wadah Altif** and her **Shura Altif**; the Christian Science Monitor and
Worldcrunch give no surname at all.

**Three things favour Cohen:**

1. The Globe and Mail is the only source that names the father at all, and names
   a specific, checkable person.
2. The ages agree across six years — 45 in 2007, 50 in 2013.
3. **Their son is called Eliazar.** Naming a son for his paternal grandfather is
   the ordinary Samaritan practice, and *Eliazar* is *Elazar*. If the boy's
   grandfather is High Priest **Elazar** ben Tsedaka, the name follows.

**And the father is somebody this project already holds.** High Priest Elazar Ben
Tzadaka is **`Elazar XX /ben Tsedaka ben Yitzhaq/`**, b. 1927 d. 2010, Geni
`6000000178794668840`, Wikidata **`Q2164896`** ("Samaritan high priest
(1927–2010)"), High Priest 2004–2010. He is one of the 33.

**This is probably the anonymous case in the CS Monitor.** That paper quotes
"Yousef Cohen, a Samaritan priest whose son recently married a Ukrainian" without
naming either — but "recently" in 2014 does not fit a 2003 arrival, so it may be
a second, later priestly marriage. **NEEDS-INVESTIGATION**, and the discriminator
is whether Yousef Cohen and Elazar ben Tsedaka are the same man (they are not —
Elazar died in 2010 and Yousef was quoted alive in 2014).

## What Geni holds about any of this: nothing

- **Elazar XX ben Tsedaka has no children recorded on Geni at all.** So Wada is
  absent, and so is any sibling.
- The current High Priest **Aabed-El V ben Asher ben Matzliach**
  (`6000000178795554821`) has four children recorded and **all four are `NN`** —
  two sons and two daughters, none named.
- No Ukrainian wife, no Israeli wife, no marriage date, anywhere in the priestly
  tree.

So the press record and the genealogy do not overlap by a single person on the
women's side.

## Worldcrunch adds money, a third name for Wadah, and two more men

Read from Emma's saved page. It is translated from a French original and keeps
detail the wire copy dropped.

**Wadah is called `Wadah Abd Moïne` here** — a third form, after `Wadah Cohen`
(RFE/RL) and `Wadah Altif` (AP). And note what it collides with: the Times of
Israel gives his son as **Eliazar, "also called Abdul Muin"**. *Abd Moïne* and
*Abdul Muin* are the same name transliterated twice, so the element is running in
the family and at least one paper has attached it to the wrong generation.
**Four spellings across four outlets for one man** — which is the reason this
project keys on profile IDs and not names.

His own account of why he married out, standing in his living room:

> "In his small living room, two ostensibly mentally disabled 50-something men
> are snoozing. They are his brothers: '**I didn't want my children to suffer
> from genetic diseases**,' as he points at them, 'so I went to Ukraine and met
> Alexandra.'"

That corroborates RFE/RL's two deaf-mute brothers from a second reporter. Wadah
himself **walks with crutches**.

**The marriages were bought, and the price is on the record.** The Tel Aviv
agency asked Wadah **$25,000**; **Azzam Altif, 55, paid $28,000**, met 17
Ukrainian women and chose **Alla Evdokimova, 26**.

**Eight** Ukrainian women, born in **Kiev and Kherson**, had reached Kiryat Luza
and Holon by then — against "about 10" and "eleven" elsewhere, so the count in
any given article is a snapshot, not a total.

**Two more named men**, both useful:

- **Rajai Altif**, who worries that most newborns that year were male. This is
  almost certainly the **Raghai** married to **Lena** in the Globe and Mail, which
  gives that couple a surname.
- **Abdallah Wasser Al-Samiri**, called the new high priest — i.e. **Aabed-El**
  ben Asher ben Matzliach, 133rd, whose four children are the `NN` records on
  Geni. And the community spokesman **Ishak Al-Samiri**.

The sex ratio here is given as **two to one**, against three to one in the
Christian Century and CS Monitor. Genetic testing is now described as
**mandatory**.

## Yousef Sadaka HaCohen — the fuller name, and still no sons

**Christian Century, Michele Chabin, 22 November 2017** (Religion News Service),
read from Emma's saved page. This is very likely the "Yousef Cohen" the CS
Monitor quoted in 2014 — a priest on Mount Gerizim — and it gives a fuller name:
**Yousef Sadaka HaCohen**.

- **802 Samaritans** worldwide in 2017; about **350** on Mount Gerizim, the rest
  near Tel Aviv, meeting several times a year.
- *"Recently, Samaritan priests embraced **genetic testing** and began allowing
  men to marry women of Ukrainian ancestry (at the time there were **three times
  more men than women**)."*
- Brides come after spending a few days with the prospective husband; after a
  trial period, *"any woman who is unhappy here is allowed to go home."*

**It names neither his wife nor any son**, so the CS Monitor lead closes here.

**`Sadaka` is a patronymic, and it suggests a check.** *Yousef ben Sadaka
ha-Cohen* would make him the son of a **Tsedaka** in the priestly line. Geni holds
`Tsedaka ben Yitzhaq` (`6000000178795067857`, `Q135489725`) with exactly one
recorded child, **Elazar XX**. If Yousef is another son he is Elazar XX's
brother — but that is inference from a name, which this repo does not do
unaided. **NEEDS-INVESTIGATION**, and the test is a source that gives Yousef's
father, not a closer look at the spelling.

## Samaritan marriage law, from the Jewish Women's Archive

The one source written **about the women**, and the only place the rules are set
out rather than described in passing. Read from Emma's saved page.

**There is exactly one priest-specific marriage rule, and it is not endogamy:**

> "Cousin marriages are permitted and may be said to be the Samaritan norm. **The
> marriage of a priest and a divorcée is forbidden.**"

So the earlier question — whether priests must marry within the priestly family —
is answered no. Nothing confines them beyond the divorcée rule, which is why
Wadah Cohen could marry a Ukrainian woman at all.

Also forbidden: brother–sister, niece or nephew, a wife's sister, a husband's
brother. Levirate marriage where a husband dies childless.

**The gene pool opens one way only:** *"Opening of the gene pool is structured to
admit **no male** from outside the Samaritan community."* Women may enter; men
may not. That is the structural reason every foreign spouse in this report is a
wife, and it means the patriline is closed by design — which matters for what
this project can ever add.

**A second wife is permitted in exactly one circumstance** — the first wife's
barrenness, minimum one year, at the High Priest's discretion. A third is not
permitted. Historically **Salamah b. Jacob** is alleged to have married seven
times; all seven wives died.

**The priest can void a betrothal or marriage against the woman's will** if the
husband is overseas and incommunicado, because of the shortage of women.

**Demography, which is the whole engine of the Ukrainian marriages:**

| year | total | female | male |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1969 | 414 | 187 | 227 |
| 1990 | 544 | 244 | 300 |

**A named Jewish marriage from the 1920s, which predates everything else here:**

> "there was a serious controversy in the 1920s when **Yafet Tsedaqa**, the
> community head, married a Jewish woman, **Miriam Chaikin**."

That is a named woman, a named husband, a decade and a documented controversy —
and Yefet ben Abraham is already in `gedcom/samaritan-sources.ged`. Whether the
community head of the 1920s is that Yefet needs checking rather than assuming.

**Women attached to priestly families in the chronicles**, all of them worth
knowing about before anyone creates items: the unnamed **daughter of Sanballat**
who married the grandson of High Priest Joiada — the same marriage our GEDCOM
records against **Manasseh**; **Miriam, daughter of High Priest Eleazar**, who
fled Nablus to avoid a marriage arranged after her father's death; the unnamed
**daughter of High Priest Amram**, said to have written a Torah scroll; and
**Sul**, maidservant of High Priest Nathaniel, who had an affair with his son
**Bahaam**. Also **Malthace the Samaritan**, one of Herod's ten wives and mother
of Archelaus and Herod Antipas.

## Unresolved

- **Whether Wadah is Saloum's nephew through a brother or more loosely.**
  "Uncle" in a community this intermarried can be a father's cousin. The test is
  a source naming Wadah's father.
- **Tanya.** Al Jazeera (2013) has a Ukrainian **Tanya** married to **Kamel**,
  pregnant at the time of writing; the CS Monitor (2014) has **Tanya Onischenko**
  married to **Tami Altif**. One woman with the husband's name given two ways, or
  two different Tanyas — not resolvable from these two pieces.
