What this does not say, and an earlier version of it did The first cut of this report sorted people into trunk / junction / corridor by how many distinct step-depths they were reached at, on the reasoning that several depths meant several branches converging. That was measuring the searcher, not the graph. Geni returns one shortest chain per destination per kind, and every chain starts at the same person, so depth is distance from that root and is near enough fixed: 
77,596  appear at exactly one depth           one shortest path each
 4,646  vary only between blood and in-law    two searches, not two branches
 1,199  vary within a single kind             and mostly by 1-3 steps
So there is no hub-versus-trunk distinction to be had here. What follows ranks by reach instead.
Reach, and why it is worth having Reach is the number of destinations whose chain passes through a person. It is a fact about the shortest-path tree rather than the underlying graph — and that is what makes it useful. A high-reach person is a cut vertex on the routes we actually have: if their record is wrong, or they come unconnected, every path behind them goes with them. Berengária of Portugal carries a tenth of everything found. 6,184 people appear on both a blood and an in-law chain, which is the one split the data genuinely offers.
