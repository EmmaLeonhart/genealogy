# The 33 Samaritan high priests: the mothers, and the wall above Tabia

The remaining disconnected component is **33 people**, the Samaritan
high-priestly line. Two ways out were checked: **the mothers**, which would link
sideways into other families, and **upward past Tabia**, which would eventually
reach recorded ground. Both are closed, and the research below is what closes
them.

## The mothers do not exist in any of the four places they could

**Geni: 0 of 33.** Not one person in the component has a mother, and there is not
one wife among them. Every marriage in eleven generations is unrecorded.

**Wikidata: 0 of 18.** Eighteen of the 33 carry a QID and all eighteen are in the
local store. Sixteen carry `P22` father. **None carries `P25` mother. None
carries `P26` spouse.** That includes the modern ones with full biographies —
Saloum Cohen `Q2067443`, Elazar ben Tsedaka `Q2164896`, Amram IX `Q107534557`.

**The community's own genealogy: omitted by construction.** The Israelite
Samaritan Information Institute publishes the family genealogies, and they are
strictly father-to-son. Women appear in photograph captions, not in the
descent — the page names exactly one, Gila Marchiv, as a wedding guest. Even
where a woman is structurally unavoidable the text goes around her: describing
the 1787 near-extinction, when a four-year-old, Shalma ben Tabia, was the sole
survivor of his father's death, it does not name the mother who kept him alive.
The tradition records patrilineal descent and nothing else.

**Press coverage of out-marriages: one first name.** Marriage outside the
community is documented but thinly. Six Samaritan men married Jewish women
between 1923 and 1969, the first a Russian immigrant, permitted because men
outnumbered marriageable women. More recently, with a 3-to-1 ratio, the priests
allowed marriages to Ukrainian women. The only wife named anywhere in that
coverage is **Alexandra**, who married Yair, a son of the high priest — a given
name, no surname, no family, no genealogy.

So the mothers are not missing from our data. They were never written down.

## And upward is blocked at exactly one profile

**1624 is the break.** Shalma I ben Phinehas, last high priest of the line of
**Eleazar son of Aaron**, died without male succession. The office passed to a
family claiming descent from Aaron's other son **Ithamar** — the *Åbtå* order,
whose first high priest was **Tsedaka II ben Tabia ha'Åbtå'i**, the 113th, our
Tabia's son.

**Nothing names Tabia's father.** Not Geni, not Wikidata — `Q135489922` has no
`P22` — and not the literature. The Åbtå descent from Ithamar is stated as a
claim with no intermediate ancestors at all.

**The chronicle that would carry it covers the other dynasty.** The *Tolidah*,
the oldest Samaritan historical work — compiled 1149 by Eleazar ben 'Amram of
Nablus, continued by Jacob ben Ishmael in 1346, extended by later scribes to
1859 — records the high-priestly succession, and the succession it records is the
**Eleazar** line. The Åbtå family's own ascent is precisely the part it does not
cover, because they were not the priestly house while it was being written.

So "go far enough up to reach recorded ground" fails at the first step rather
than partway: there is no second step to take.
