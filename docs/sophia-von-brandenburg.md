# Sophia von Brandenburg (1450–1480) and the Underberge family: 2026-10-09

**Why she matters:** she is the one dead end among Emma's ancestors whose name points at Abul
Hamza's descendants (`dead-end-candidates.md`). In the synoptic tree she is 16 generations above
Emma, has no parents, and is married to Gard Toreson Underberge II (b. 1454); their sons are Halvor
(1475), Tore (1481) and Osmund (1485) Gardson Underberge. If she was a Brandenburg (Hohenzollern)
princess, she is the link.

## What was found

- **No source connects a Sophia von Brandenburg to Underberge.** Web searches in English, German
  and Norwegian found only other Sophias of Brandenburg (Brandenburg-Stendal c. 1300–1356, the
  Ansbach and Kulmbach Sophias of the 1600s–1700s, Queen Sophie Magdalene of Denmark-Norway), none
  married into Norway in the 1400s.
- **None of Albrecht III Achilles' 17 children in the synoptic tree is a Sophia**, and no other
  Hohenzollern of the right age in the tree is named as her parent.
- **Underberge is a real farm and family:** Under-Berge in Høgsfjord (Høle, now Sandnes,
  Rogaland), seat of the "Berge-ætta". The published work on that family is Sigleif Engen,
  "Berge-ætta fra Høgsfjord i Ryfylke", *Lagnaden* 1942 (pp. 40–142), and Eyvin Dahl, "Gard
  Toresons hustru: Ramborg Knutsdotter", *Lagnaden* 1946 (pp. 13–46). Those concern Gard Toreson
  (lagmann in Ryfylke, d. c. 1427) and Ramborg Knutsdotter, said to be a natural daughter of Knut
  Algotsson; their son Tore Gardson is called the ancestor of the Under-Berge family. Gard was the
  main farmer at Under-Berge in 1519 and 1521 (a later Gard). Nothing found names a Brandenburg
  wife in that family.

## Reading

A Brandenburg princess marrying a Rogaland farmer in the 1470s would be recorded somewhere on the
Hohenzollern side, and nothing is. The most likely reading is that "von Brandenburg" on this
profile is a family tradition or a genealogy-site attribution without a source, not a documented
princess. That is a reading, not a finding: the *Lagnaden* articles were not seen in full, and
the original Geni profile's notes are still to be read from the exports on disk.

Whether to attach her to the Hohenzollerns anyway is Emma's call (where a line attaches in the
story). Nothing has been applied anywhere.

## Page 64 read (2026-10-10, from Emma's purchased copy)

Scan page 64 is book page 58, chapter "Lagmannsætta", the ancestry of Ramborg Knutsdotter. The one
"Brandenburg" is:

> "11. Knut Valdemarson (12.. – 1260 15/10). Knut var dansk prins … Han vart hertug av Estland og
> Reval … Knut var gift to gonger, først med Hedvig av Brandenburg, og han hadde med henne sonen
> Erik … Andre gongen var han gift med Ingegerd av Pommern, og med henne hadde han sonen

**So it is not about Sophia.** The Brandenburg woman is Hedvig, Knut Valdemarsson's first wife, and
she is not even in this line (the line runs through his second wife, Ingegerd of Pomerania).
*Forsandboka* gives no source for a Sophia von Brandenburg in the Underberge family. Her name
still rests only on FamilySearch tree person LKM3-Z4V.

**What the page does give:** the book's line for Ramborg Knutsdotter, wife of Gard Toreson on
Talgje and ancestress of the Under-Berge family: Valdemar II Sejr of Denmark (1170–1241) → Knut
Valdemarsson → Svantepolk Knutsson (Lejon) → Ingegerd Svantepolksdotter (m. Brynjulf Bengtsson) →
Algot Brynjulfsson (Lejon) → Knut Algotsson (Lejon), m. Märta Ulfsdotter → Ramborg Knutsdotter.
The book also gives Ramborg's mother as **Märta Ulfsdotter, daughter of Ulf Gudmarsson and Saint
Birgitta of Sweden** ("Morsætta til Ramborg Knutsdotter").

**Against the trees:**
- This royal line is **already among Emma's synoptic-tree ancestors** (Gard Toreson Underberge II →
  Tore Gardsson → Gard Toresson and Ramborg Knutsdotter Lejon → Knut Algotsson → … → Svantepolk
  Knutsson → Valdemar II, 24 generations above Emma), through Gard's own line, not through Sophia.
- The synoptic tree gives Ramborg's mother as "NN (Frille)" (an unnamed concubine), not Märta
  Ulfsdotter. *Forsandboka* would make Saint Birgitta Emma's ancestor. Märta Ulfsdotter (1319)
  is in the synoptic tree with Ulf Gudmarsson and Birgitta as parents, but not linked to Ramborg.
- **Neither line is among Abul Hamza's descendants**, in the RootsMagic GEDCOM or in his

## Two readings to keep apart (2026-10-10)

- **Ramborg's mother is disputed, not simply wrong.** Knut Algotsson did marry Märta Ulfsdotter
  (b. c. 1319). Sollied (1941) argued Ramborg was Knut's illegitimate daughter (the synoptic tree's
  "NN (Frille)"); Engen (1942) left her legitimacy open; *Forsandboka* (1985) names Märta. Märta
  would have been about 40 at Ramborg's birth (c. 1360). The article that decides it is Eyvin Dahl,
  "Gard Toresons hustru: Ramborg Knutsdotter", *Lagnaden* 1946, not yet seen.
- **Where "von Brandenburg" may have come from (a guess, no source says so):** the family's printed
  history names a Brandenburg woman, Hedvig, wife of Knut Valdemarsson (d. 1260), on a side branch.
  A tree built from that book could have carried the name onto Gard II Toreson's unknown wife, about
  200 years and seven generations later.

## A real, small-scale "von Brandenburg" in western Norway at the right time (2026-10-10)

Emma's question: might there be someone named von Brandenburg alive then, on a smaller scale than
the margraves? Diplomatarium Norvegicum full-text search (dokpro.uio.no, 1300–1550; 346 hits, almost
all the ruling house, among them Queen Dorothea of Brandenburg, queen of Denmark and Norway from
1445) gave one strong answer, in the papal registers printed in **DN XVII**:

- Sixtus IV, 4th year (1474–75): a petition "for the cleric **Johannes v. Brandenburg** of the
  diocese of Bamberg, **uægte Søn af Markgreven af Brandenburg** [illegitimate son of the Margrave
  of Brandenburg], to obtain the **archdeaconry of Bergen** and a canonry and prebend in Nidaros"
  (Supplicationes Sixti IV an. 4 lib. 7, fol. 81).
- **DN XVII 719, 7 November 1480:** Johannes von Brandenburg resigns the Bergen archdeaconry to
  Henrik Pelrenhorst.

Also found: Johannes Brandenburg, merchant in Yarmouth, and Henrik Brandenburg, burgher of Hamburg,
in a 1403 Stralsund record about a ship of a man who died in Bergen. These are merchants with the
surname, and too early for Sophia.

**What this means for Sophia, all inference:** Bamberg diocese is Franconia, the Franconian
Hohenzollerns' land, where Albrecht Achilles was margrave until 1486, so Johannes is likely his
illegitimate son or a near relative's. An illegitimate child of the margrave was called plainly
"von Brandenburg", the same form as "Sophia von Brandenburg". The archdeacon was in western Norway
in the mid-1470s, when Sophia's sons were born (1475, 1481, 1485). Nothing names her, and
Høgsfjord is in Stavanger diocese, not Bergen's. But a Brandenburg connection in this family is no
longer implausible: an illegitimate Hohenzollern daughter, or a relative of the Bergen
archdeacon, is a possible reading. Next places to look: the Bergen archdeacon's household and
relatives; whether Albrecht Achilles had an illegitimate daughter Sophia; Stavanger diocese records
of the 1470s.

## The archdeacon identified (2026-10-10)

**DN XVII 685, 2 December 1474, Rome** ([dokpro.uio.no](https://www.dokpro.uio.no/cgi-bin/middelalder/diplom_vise_tekst.cgi?b=14767&s=n&str=)),
from *Supplicationes Sixti IV* an. 4 lib. 7 (943) fol. 81. Cardinal Francesco Gonzaga petitions for
"Johannis Brandeburgensis clerici Bambergensis diocesis, avunculi sui, de nobili marchionum genere
procreati, qui in vicesimo tertio vel circa sue etatis anno constitutus existit et defectum
natalium patitur, de coniugato marchione et soluta genitus" to have the archdeaconry of Bergen and
a canonry in Nidaros. **DN XVII 719, 7 November 1480** ([dokpro.uio.no](https://www.dokpro.uio.no/cgi-bin/middelalder/diplom_vise_tekst.cgi?b=14801&s=n&str=)):
he resigns them to Henrik Pelrenhorst.

So Johannes was **about 23 in December 1474 (born c. 1451)**, the son of a married margrave and an
unmarried woman, and the cardinal's *avunculus* (mother's brother). Cardinal Francesco Gonzaga's
mother was Barbara of Brandenburg, daughter of **Johann "the Alchemist", Margrave of
Brandenburg-Kulmbach** (from memory, to be checked), which matches Bamberg diocese (Kulmbach). So
Johannes is most likely Johann the Alchemist's illegitimate son, and Barbara's half-brother. Johann
the Alchemist was Albrecht Achilles' brother, with the same ancestry, which reaches Abul Hamza.

**For Sophia:** born c. 1451, Johannes cannot be her father. He could be her brother; nothing shows
it. If Sophia were an illegitimate daughter of Johann the Alchemist, she would be the link to Abul
Hamza. Next: Johann the Alchemist's known natural children.

## Correction: he probably never took up the post (2026-10-10)

The full Latin of **DN XVII 719 (7 November 1480)** says the Pope had provided "Jo. de Brandeborch
clerico [Bambergensis] diocesis" with (1) the archdeaconry of Bergen and the prebend in Nidaros
(grant dated 2 December 1474) and (2) the provostship of the Benedictine nunnery "Lippie" in Verden
diocese (grant dated 27 August 1477), and that he now gives these up "concessionem gratie huiusmodi
litteris apostolicis super ea non confectis": **the papal letters executing the grants had never
been drawn up.** He resigns in favour of Henrik Pelrenhorst, a priest who had spent nearly twenty
years at the Curia. Values: archdeaconry and canonry at most 20 gold florins a year each; the
provostship at most 20 marks of silver. "Concessum ut petitur."

So Johannes's Bergen appointment was a paper provision arranged in Rome by his nephew Cardinal
Gonzaga, and given up before it took effect. **He was very likely never in Norway.** The earlier
sections overstate this ("in western Norway in the mid-1470s"); this section corrects them. What
stands: an illegitimate Hohenzollern born c. 1451, of Sophia's generation, held a claim on a
Norwegian church office in 1474–1480. That is a much weaker link to Sophia than first written.

## Johann the Alchemist's family reaches Norway: Queen Dorothea (2026-10-10)

- **Legitimate children of Johann "the Alchemist"** (de.wikipedia mirror; Deutsche Biographie):
  Barbara (Marchioness of Mantua), Rudolf, Elisabeth, **Dorothea**.
- **Dorothea of Brandenburg (1430–1495), queen of Denmark, Norway and Sweden** (Wikidata Q61873:
  father John, Margrave of Brandenburg-Kulmbach; mother Barbara of Saxe-Wittenberg; husbands
  Christopher of Bavaria, 1445, and Christian I, 1449). DN XVII also has her morning-gift from
  King Christopher (15 September 1445), which assigned her Jämtland in Norway among other lands.
- **Natural children:** Pope Pius II mentions an illegitimate son of Johann in 1458 (Deutsche
  Biographie); a "Fritz von Brandenburg", illegitimate son, had daughters Magdalena and Franziska who
  married Italian counts (single source, a de.wikipedia mirror). Plus Johannes (b. c. 1451), the
  Bergen archdeacon of 1474–1480, per DN XVII 685.

**Reading (inference, no document names Sophia):** Johann the Alchemist's legitimate daughter was
queen of Norway from 1445 to 1481 and queen dowager until 1495, and his natural children were
provided for through the family's connections (the Gonzaga cardinal for Johannes; Italian marriages
for Fritz's daughters). An illegitimate daughter of his born c. 1450, placed through her half-sister
the queen, marrying into a Norwegian lawman's family, is a plausible origin for "Sophia von
Brandenburg". If she was his daughter, Emma descends from Abul Hamza through her (Johann the
Alchemist is among his descendants in the synoptic tree). Next: Queen Dorothea's household and
ladies; Norwegian or Danish records of a "Sophie/Sofie" with the queen; Stavanger-area marriages
of the 1470s.

**Not the same man:** Wikidata Q69437231 / Catholic Hierarchy "brandej", Johann von Brandenburg,
bishop-elect of Płock (appointed 26 September 1522, d. 1523, *Hierarchia Catholica* III 276), is
Johann Albrecht of Brandenburg-Ansbach ("Jan Albrecht", b. 1499), brother of Grand Master Albrecht
of Prussia, nominated after Erazm Ciołek's death (Tygodnik Ciechanowski, "Brat wielkiego mistrza
biskupem w Płocku?"). Legitimate, and born about 50 years after the Bergen Johannes (c. 1451).

**Diplomatarium Norvegicum checked for her (2026-10-10):** full-text search for "Sophi*" (76 hits)
and "Sofi*" (146 hits) over 1300–1550: **none dated in the 1440s–1490s.** No Sophie appears in

## Fritz, another natural son, and the pattern (2026-10-10, found by Emma)

**Fritz di Brandeburgo** ([Wikidata Q98479306](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q98479306);
[it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_di_Brandeburgo)): "figlio naturale di Giovanni
l'Alchimista"; mother NN. He "si stabilì presso la corte dei Gonzaga di Mantova della sorellastra
Barbara". Daughters: Maddalena, married Giberto Borromeo (1463–1508), count of Arona; Francesca
(d. 1512), married Borso da Correggio, count of Correggio.

**The pattern:** Johann the Alchemist's natural children were placed through his legitimate
daughters' courts. Fritz went to Barbara's court at Mantua, and his daughters married Italian
counts. Johannes got the Bergen claim through Barbara's son, Cardinal Gonzaga. His other legitimate
daughter, Dorothea, was queen of Denmark and Norway. A natural daughter placed through Dorothea
would be married off in Scandinavia as plain "von Brandenburg", which is how Sophia's profile reads.
This is the strongest argument for Sophia so far, and it is still circumstantial: no document names
her.

**The Underberge family's standing (Emma, 2026-10-10: "they're not nothing ... they are
surprisingly high up"):** *Forsandboka* calls them "Lagmannsætta". Gard Toreson was lawman
(lagmann) of Ryfylke; his wife Ramborg Knutsdotter was of the Swedish Lejon family, descended from
Valdemar II of Denmark, and per *Forsandboka* a granddaughter of Saint Birgitta. For a margrave's
natural daughter placed through the queen, a marriage into a lawman's family of royal and noble
descent is a fitting rank: above a farm family, below the princely marriages that leave records.
This makes the Sophia reading more plausible; it does not document it.
