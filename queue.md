- **Open question, for the end: when did equivalent European names become separate names? (Emma, 2026-10-04).** The namosphere rule (names match across languages as one name) holds historically, but names that were once the same have drifted apart, and not consistently. Emma's reading: first a nationalist period when countries distanced themselves from the pan-European forms, then English-centred internationalization, where people meet a foreign person under their native form, so today effectively every name is its own thing.
