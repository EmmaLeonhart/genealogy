- **Open question, for the end: when did equivalent European names become separate names? (Emma, 2026-10-04).** The namosphere rule (names match across languages as one name) holds historically, but names that were once the same have drifted apart, and not consistently. Hans may have become a name separate from Johan(n). Yuri, George and Jørgen used to be one name and are now clearly different. Emma's reading: first a nationalist period when countries distanced themselves from the pan-European forms, then English-centred internationalization, where people meet a foreign person under their native form, so today effectively every name is its own thing. Monarchs keep the old shared forms somewhat, but not always, and a culture can even split a name retroactively: the French count Clovis and Louis as separate names with separate numbering, though they are the same name. Think through what this means for judging identities by period (old equivalence vs modern distinctness) and for the given-name items we link, write the result into `CLAUDE.md` as a written principle (never code), and ask Emma with AskUserQuestion about any specific case it doesn't settle.
