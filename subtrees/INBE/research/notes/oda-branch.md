# The Oda as an Inbe branch

Started 2026-10-03. The user asked (2026-10-02): "the Oda clan is an Inbe clan.
It is a hemp clan branch, is that correct?"

## Short answer

**Possibly, but the evidence is thin, and it is all later family pedigrees.**
The Oda took their name from Oda in Echizen, where they were connected with
**Tsurugi Shrine** (劔神社, Oda Myōjin). The idea that they descend from that
shrine's Inbe priests rests on pedigrees kept by local shrine families, whose
date is unknown. The earliest real Oda document has them signing as
**Fujiwara**. Their founder Chikazane is first named in **1641**.

## The evidence in order of date

| Date | Evidence | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1290 | Five-ring stupa at Hōraku-ji (Echizen town) | Grave of **親尊 Chikataka**, d. Shōō 3.2.19. Announced in 2011 as Chikazane's (親真) and corrected by the town's 2012 survey. Not evidence for Chikazane |
| 1393 (Meitoku 4) | 「藤原信昌・将広置文」, a written disposition kept at Tsurugi Shrine | The earliest securely Oda-related document. The signatories call themselves **Fujiwara** (Nobumasa and Masahiro). Wikidata gives 藤原将広 as a possible father of the Oda head 織田常松 (`data_lake/downloads/wikidata/father_chain_oda_nobunaga.txt`) |
| 1549 | Nobunaga, aged 16 | Signs as "**藤原**信長" |
| 1570s | Nobunaga | Claims **Taira** descent (from Shigemori) |
| c. 1611 | Oze Hoan, 『信長記』 | Taira story (Sukemori's concubine, Tsuda in Ōmi, adoption by the Tsurugi priests); **no name for the founder** |
| 1641/1658 | Hayashi Razan, 『織田信長譜』 (ordered by Iemitsu) | **Chikazane first named**, as Taira Sukemori's son |
| c. 1692 | 『続群書類従』 「織田系図」 | The standard Taira pedigree |
| unknown | 『忌部氏系譜』, 『越前織田明神社家忌部上坂系譜』 (Inbe Kōsaka family of the Oda Myōjin priests), 『織田氏系譜』, held by Oda shrine families | Chikazane's father is **忌部親澄, 神祇権大祐** (d. Jōei 2.3.18, 1233). Chikazane became priest of Oda Myōjin in 1233 and died Shōgen 2.2.18 (1260). "**平氏にあらず、忌部神主の正系なり**" ("not Taira, the direct line of the Inbe priests") |
| Meiji | Nakata Norinobu, 「織田右大臣信長公ノ系統及履歴」 | Extends the Inbe line up from Chikazumi through Fumiyama to Hamanari. This is FamilySearch's 14 generations (see `familysearch-survey.md`) |

Sources: Echizen town 『広報えちぜん』 Sept. 2012 (in
`data_lake/downloads/echizen/`); Echizen Town Oda Cultural History Museum,
panel 03 <https://www.town.echizen.fukui.jp/otabunreki/panel/03.html>
(accessed 2026-10-03); ja Wikipedia 平親真 and 斎部文山.

## Reading it

- Both the Taira and the Fujiwara claims are agreed to be later status
  claims. Nobunaga changed from one to the other within his own lifetime.
- The Inbe claim is the local one. It is tied to the shrine the Oda really
  were connected with, and is held by the shrine families themselves. That
  makes it more plausible than the Taira story, but no securely dated
  document supports it yet.
- *Open:* when were the 忌部上坂系譜 and 忌部氏系譜 written? If they turn out
  to be Edo or Meiji, the Oda–Inbe link is in the same position as the other
  Inbe pedigrees. *Also open:* whether the 1393 Fujiwara signatories were
  the shrine's priests.
