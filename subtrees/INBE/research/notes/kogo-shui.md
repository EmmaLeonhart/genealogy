# Kogo Shūi (807): the Inbe's own account of their genealogy

Written by **Inbe no Hironari** and presented to Emperor Heizei in 807 to
argue the Inbe case against the Nakatomi. Text consulted 2026-10-02 through
nihonsinwa.com (section 2, 天中の三神と氏祖系譜) and a transcription with
readings on note.com (masachan5). *To check against Nishimiya Kazutami's
Iwanami Bunko edition.*

## What it gives

**The god layer, in one generation:**

- 高皇産靈神 **Takamimusubi** has three children: a daughter 栲幡千千姫命
  (Takuhatachijihime), 天忍日命 (Ame no Oshihi, ancestor of the Ōtomo), and
  **天太玉命 Ame no Futodama**, "ancestor of the Inbe no Sukune" (齋部宿禰祖也).
- Futodama "leads" (所率) five gods, each the ancestor of a regional group:

  | God | Group |
  |---|---|
  | 天日鷲命 Ame no Hiwashi | Awa Inbe (阿波國忌部) |
  | 手置帆負命 Taokiho-oi | Sanuki Inbe |
  | 彦狭知命 Hikosashiri | Kii Inbe |
  | 櫛明玉命 Kushiakarutama | Izumo jewel-makers (玉作) |
  | 天目一箇命 Ama no Mahitotsu | Tsukushi and Ise Inbe |

  These gods are **not** called Futodama's children or kin. They are his
  followers. *Inference:* the Inbe's own 807 account presents the regional
  groups as a working organisation under Futodama, not as one bloodline. That
  is the "not all blood relatives" point the user raised, and it comes from
  the Inbe themselves.

**One extra generation, in the Jimmu story:**

- "天富命（太玉命の孫）": **Ame no Tomi, Futodama's grandson**, leads the
  descendants (孫) of Taokiho-oi and Hikosashiri to build the palace. Then he
  "takes part of the Awa Inbe east" to plant hemp and paper-mulberry, founds
  Futodama's shrine there (now Awa Shrine), and the area is named **Awa**
  (安房) after the Awa Inbe. The Shikoku Awa Inbe's district is called **麻殖
  (Oe, "hemp-planting")** for the same reason, and they still send bark cloth
  and hemp cloth for the Daijōsai.
- Ame no Tomi's father is **not named**. Ame no Kushimimi appears only in
  later sources.

## What it does not give

- **No chain from any god to any living Inbe**, not even to Hironari himself.
  The Inbe's own advocate, writing to prove his clan's ancient standing, gives
  the ancestor god, a grandson in Jimmu's time, and then nothing.
- No generations between Ame no Hiwashi and the Awa Inbe of 807, and none for
  the Sanuki, Kii or other groups.

## What this means for the question

The two earliest Inbe sources, the Kogo Shūi (807) and the Shinsen Shōjiroku
(815, see `shinsen-shojiroku.md`), agree. The Inbe claimed descent from
Futodama and gave a regional group structure under him, with **no
generation-by-generation pedigree**. A clan in a public fight over its
standing had every reason to produce a pedigree if it had one. *Inference:*
this suggests the Inbe of 807 had no pedigree to produce, or none worth
showing. The pedigrees first appear in 1870.
